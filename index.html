I work at Armadin, where I help build agentic swarms for cybersecurity, and the measurement science of figuring out what they can actually do.

I recently graduated from Stanford, where I studied math and ancient history.

Research

I want to uncover the general principles behind scaling large AI systems: what capabilities emerge, and how we can keep these systems secure and robust.

I like working on concrete problems, such as backdoor detection, isolation, and containment, as well as fuzzier ones: what are the learning dynamics of these systems, and what happens once they outgrow our ability to check them?

I worked on Archon (ICML 2025), a framework for test-time compute, in Chris Ré’s HazyResearch, and did research on robotics in Dorsa Sadigh’s ILIAD lab.

History

I’m drawn to social and economic history, especially the Roman empire, and to the limits of institutions more broadly. Recently, I have been thinking about:

  • What informal networks let strangers from opposite ends of the ancient world collaborate at scale (e.g. collegia)?
  • What are the limits of rigid institutions (e.g. magistrates and distance), and how do they adapt?
  • How do social orders build trust without verification (e.g. Mediterranean trading communities)?
  • Why did Rome never industrialize, and why did it underperform at innovation? What makes a society great at innovating?

I wrote an essay on how collegia, Rome’s voluntary associations, shaped Roman social life, and what they tell us about institutional adaptation.

Personal

I grew up in the cherry capital of Bulgaria. Studying math there built a deep appreciation for simple, intuition-heavy “tricks” for solving problems, a philosophy that still shapes the open questions in AI and science I care about. It’s also where I did some of my first research, on adversarial robustness, back in high school.

In my free time, I love reading books, skiing, and playing tennis.
